HVAC PERMIT REQUIREMENTS
City of Phoenix Mechanical Division
Document Reference: HVAC-2026-01

================================================================
SECTION 1: GENERAL HVAC REQUIREMENTS
================================================================

1.1 Applicable Codes and Standards
All HVAC system designs must comply with:
- International Mechanical Code (IMC) 2021 as adopted by Phoenix
- ASHRAE Standard 90.1-2019 (Energy Standard for Buildings)
- ASHRAE Standard 62.1-2019 (Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality)
- International Energy Conservation Code (IECC) 2021
- Phoenix Mechanical Code local amendments
- ASHRAE Standard 55-2020 (Thermal Environmental Conditions)

1.2 Climate Zone
Phoenix is located in ASHRAE/IECC Climate Zone 2B (Hot-Dry).
All energy efficiency requirements and equipment minimums are based on this climate zone classification.

1.3 Licensed Professional Requirement
HVAC system designs for commercial buildings must be prepared by a licensed mechanical engineer registered in the State of Arizona. The engineer's stamp and signature must appear on all mechanical drawing sheets.

SECTION 2: HVAC PLAN SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS
================================================================

2.1 Required HVAC Drawings
A complete HVAC plan submission must include:

(a) Mechanical Floor Plans
- Equipment layout showing all HVAC units, air handlers, and terminal units
- Ductwork layout with sizes (supply, return, exhaust, outside air)
- Diffuser and grille layout with sizes and airflow quantities
- Refrigerant piping layout (for split systems and VRF)
- Control zone layout with thermostat locations
- Access panel locations for maintenance

(b) Mechanical Schedules
- Equipment schedule listing all HVAC units with:
  * Manufacturer and model number
  * Cooling capacity (tons or BTU/h)
  * Heating capacity (BTU/h)
  * Airflow (CFM)
  * Efficiency ratings (SEER, EER, IEER, HSPF, COP as applicable)
  * Refrigerant type and charge
  * Electrical characteristics (voltage, phase, MCA, MOCP)
  * Weight (for structural support design)
  * Sound ratings (if applicable)

(c) Mechanical Details
- Equipment mounting and support details
- Duct connection details
- Vibration isolation details
- Roof penetration details
- Fire/smoke damper details
- Access door details

(d) Control System
- Sequence of operations for each HVAC system
- Control system riser diagram
- BMS/DDC point list
- Setpoint schedules
- Economizer control strategy

2.2 Required Calculations
The following calculations must be submitted:

(a) Heating and Cooling Load Calculations
- Must be performed using ASHRAE-approved methods
- Manual J (residential) or Manual N (commercial)
- Or ASHRAE Handbook of Fundamentals methods
- Must account for all heat gains and losses
- Must use Phoenix design conditions:
  * Summer: 110°F dry bulb, 71°F wet bulb
  * Winter: 35°F dry bulb

(b) Ventilation Calculations
- Outdoor air requirements per ASHRAE 62.1
- Ventilation rate procedure or IAQ procedure
- Exhaust air quantities for kitchens, restrooms, and parking garages

(c) Duct Sizing Calculations
- Static pressure calculations for each system
- Duct sizing per equal friction or static regain method
- Fan selection based on system static pressure requirements

SECTION 3: ENERGY EFFICIENCY REQUIREMENTS (ASHRAE 90.1)
================================================================

3.1 Minimum Equipment Efficiency
All HVAC equipment must meet or exceed the minimum efficiency requirements of ASHRAE 90.1-2019 for Climate Zone 2B:

(a) Packaged Rooftop Units (Cooling)
- Less than 65,000 BTU/h: Minimum 14.0 SEER2
- 65,000 to 135,000 BTU/h: Minimum 11.2 EER
- 135,000 to 240,000 BTU/h: Minimum 11.0 EER
- Greater than 240,000 BTU/h: Minimum 10.0 EER

Note: SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) measures the ratio of cooling output to energy input over a typical cooling season. For Climate Zone 2B (Phoenix), where cooling is the dominant energy use, meeting minimum SEER requirements is critical.

(b) Split System Air Conditioners
- Less than 65,000 BTU/h: Minimum 15.0 SEER2
- 65,000 to 135,000 BTU/h: Minimum 11.2 EER

(c) Heat Pumps
- Less than 65,000 BTU/h: Minimum 15.0 SEER2 / 8.1 HSPF2
- 65,000 to 135,000 BTU/h: Minimum 11.0 EER / 3.3 COP

3.2 Economizer Requirements
Air-side economizers are required for:
- Individual cooling systems with cooling capacity greater than 54,000 BTU/h
- Exceptions per ASHRAE 90.1 Section 6.5.1.1 may apply

Economizer controls must include:
- Dry-bulb or differential enthalpy control strategy
- Integrated economizer operation (not an either/or with mechanical cooling)
- Fault detection and diagnostics (FDD) per Section 6.4.3.10

3.3 Energy Recovery Ventilation (ERV)
Energy recovery is required when:
- Design supply air quantity exceeds 5,000 CFM at design conditions, AND
- The outdoor air fraction is 70% or greater, OR
- The system provides more than 30% outdoor air

ERV system requirements:
- Minimum 50% energy recovery effectiveness
- Must include bypass capability for economizer operation
- Must not produce condensation that could damage the building

IMPORTANT: For commercial buildings in Climate Zone 2B with supply air exceeding 5,000 CFM, energy recovery ventilation is almost always required. This is a frequently missed requirement that results in permit rejection.

3.4 Duct Insulation Requirements
Minimum duct insulation R-values:

(a) Supply Ducts
- In conditioned space: R-0 (no insulation required if ΔT < 15°F)
- In unconditioned space: R-6.0 minimum
- Exterior: R-8.0 minimum
- Underground: R-6.0 minimum

(b) Return Ducts
- In conditioned space: R-0
- In unconditioned space: R-4.2 minimum
- Exterior: R-6.0 minimum

IMPORTANT: Duct insulation in unconditioned spaces (attics, mechanical rooms above ceiling) must meet the R-6.0 minimum for supply ducts. Many submissions specify R-4.2, which is insufficient for supply ducts in unconditioned spaces in Climate Zone 2B.

3.5 Piping Insulation
Refrigerant suction piping:
- All suction lines must be insulated with minimum 3/4-inch thick insulation
- Hot gas discharge lines must be insulated in accessible areas for burn protection

3.6 Controls Requirements
HVAC controls must provide:
- Independent temperature control for each zone
- Setback/setup capability (unoccupied setpoints)
- Optimum start/stop (for DDC-controlled systems)
- Demand control ventilation for spaces with design occupancy > 25 per 1,000 sq ft
- Simultaneous heating and cooling limitations per Section 6.5.2

SECTION 4: STRUCTURAL SUPPORT FOR ROOFTOP EQUIPMENT
================================================================

4.1 Rooftop Unit Support Requirements
Per Phoenix Mechanical Code Section 301.5:
- All rooftop HVAC units must be mounted on code-compliant supports
- Units weighing more than 2,000 lbs require structural engineering drawings stamped by a licensed structural engineer
- Support design must account for:
  * Equipment operating weight (including refrigerant charge)
  * Wind loads per ASCE 7
  * Seismic loads per ASCE 7 Chapter 13
  * Snow loads (minimal in Phoenix but must be addressed)
  * Vibration isolation provisions

4.2 Roof Penetration Requirements
All roof penetrations for HVAC must:
- Be properly flashed and waterproofed
- Maintain required roof fire ratings
- Include curb details (minimum 8-inch curb height)
- Be coordinated with the roofing manufacturer warranty requirements

================================================================
SECTION 5: INSPECTION REQUIREMENTS
================================================================

5.1 Required HVAC Inspections
- Underground mechanical (before backfill)
- Rough-in (ductwork and piping before concealment)
- Equipment installation
- Controls and test/balance
- Final inspection

5.2 Commissioning
For commercial buildings over 25,000 sq ft:
- HVAC systems must be commissioned per ASHRAE Standard 202
- A commissioning plan must be submitted with the permit application
- Commissioning report must be submitted before certificate of occupancy
