# Resubmission Guidelines for Construction Permits

## Overview

This document provides comprehensive guidelines for contractors and permit applicants who need to resubmit a construction permit application after a rejection, revision request, or when addressing authority comments. Following these guidelines will help ensure a smooth resubmission process and minimize further delays.

## When Resubmission Is Required

A permit resubmission is required in the following situations:

1. **Permit Rejected**: The authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) has formally rejected the permit application due to code violations, incomplete documentation, or non-compliance with local requirements.

2. **Revision Required**: The plans examiner has requested specific revisions to submitted documents, drawings, or calculations before the permit can be approved.

3. **Expired Documents**: One or more required documents (such as insurance certificates or contractor licenses) have expired since the original submission and must be renewed.

4. **Incomplete Submission**: The original submission was missing one or more required documents, and the applicant needs to provide the missing materials.

5. **Voluntary Withdrawal and Resubmission**: The applicant has voluntarily withdrawn the application to make significant design changes.

## Pre-Resubmission Checklist

Before resubmitting a permit application, complete the following checklist:

### Document Review
- [ ] Review all authority comments and rejection reasons carefully
- [ ] Identify every specific issue mentioned by the reviewer
- [ ] Verify that each issue has been addressed in the revised documents
- [ ] Ensure all required documents are included in the resubmission package
- [ ] Check that all documents are current and not expired
- [ ] Verify that all drawings bear the stamp and signature of the appropriate licensed professional

### Compliance Verification
- [ ] Confirm that revised plans comply with all applicable building codes
- [ ] Verify energy code compliance (ASHRAE 90.1, IECC as applicable)
- [ ] Check structural calculations against current load requirements
- [ ] Ensure electrical plans meet NEC 2023 requirements
- [ ] Verify mechanical plans meet applicable mechanical code requirements
- [ ] Confirm fire safety compliance with NFPA standards

### Administrative Requirements
- [ ] Include a written response to each reviewer comment explaining what changes were made
- [ ] Provide a marked-up set of drawings highlighting all changes (cloud and delta revisions)
- [ ] Update the revision block on all modified drawings
- [ ] Include a cover letter summarizing all changes made since the previous submission
- [ ] Pay any applicable resubmission fees
- [ ] Verify that the permit application form information is still current

## Common Reasons for Rejection

### Documentation Issues
- Missing required drawings (site plan, structural, architectural, MEP)
- Drawings not stamped by a licensed professional
- Incomplete or missing calculations
- Outdated reference standards cited in documents

### Code Compliance Issues
- Structural design not meeting minimum load requirements
- Electrical plans not complying with NEC 2023
- Mechanical systems not meeting ASHRAE 90.1 energy efficiency standards
- Fire protection systems not meeting NFPA 13 requirements
- Accessibility requirements not addressed per ADA and local amendments

### Insurance and Licensing Issues
- Expired general liability insurance certificate
- Insurance coverage below minimum required amounts
- Contractor license not valid for the type of work proposed
- Missing workers compensation certificate

## Response to Authority Comments

When responding to authority comments in a resubmission:

1. **Address Every Comment**: Do not skip any comment, even if you disagree with it. Provide a written response to each item.

2. **Be Specific**: Reference the specific drawing sheet, detail number, or specification section where changes were made.

3. **Provide Evidence**: Include calculations, code references, or manufacturer data to support your response where applicable.

4. **Highlight Changes**: Use revision clouds and delta symbols on drawings to make it easy for the reviewer to find changes.

5. **Maintain Professional Tone**: Keep all responses professional and factual.

## Resubmission Timeline

- **Standard Review**: Resubmissions typically receive a review within 10-15 business days
- **Expedited Review**: Some jurisdictions offer expedited review for a fee (typically 5-7 business days)
- **Multiple Resubmissions**: If a permit requires more than two resubmissions, a pre-submission conference with the AHJ is strongly recommended

## Best Practices

1. **Engage Early**: If unsure about any requirement, schedule a pre-application meeting with the AHJ
2. **Use Qualified Professionals**: Ensure all drawings are prepared by appropriately licensed professionals
3. **Review Before Submission**: Have an independent qualified professional review the complete submission package before resubmitting
4. **Track Expiration Dates**: Monitor insurance and license expiration dates and renew them proactively
5. **Maintain Communication**: Keep open communication with the plans examiner assigned to your project
6. **Document Everything**: Keep copies of all submissions, correspondence, and reviewer comments
